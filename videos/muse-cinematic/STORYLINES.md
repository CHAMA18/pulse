# STORYLINES — three different films

## A — THE FORGE ★ recommended
- **Device:** The result first. **Form:** One continuous take — the camera never cuts, it pushes, pulls and travels from one world into the next through objects on screen.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** Frame 0 is a finished, living product filling the screen. The camera pulls back through the browser chrome, through the code that made it, through the single sentence that asked for it — then dives forward again as Muse rebuilds it in front of you, verb by verb, until the payoff plays at full speed and the mark is forged from the last line of code. It feels like watching a master craftsman in a dark studio where the only light is the work itself.
- **First two seconds, exactly:** A pharmacy dashboard is already alive — orders ticking, a chart drawing itself — filling 100% of the frame. No title. At 1.2 s the camera begins a slow pull-back and the browser chrome edge enters, revealing this is a screen that was just made.
- **Beats:** result alive → pull back to the sentence → the old way churns (one held frame that rebuilds) → MUSE reveal → describe / build / refine / ship → payoff at speed → claim → forged end card.
- **Look:** Matte charcoal studio #0C0C0E with 2% grain and 6% vignette (a seamless sweep, not a void — the hero always fills ≥60% of the frame), warm bone ink #F2EDE3, one molten accent #FF5A1F rationed to one word per shot. Montserrat 800 display at 96–140 px, JetBrains Mono for code. Light is a key from upper-left, rim from behind.
- **Sound:** 120 BPM minimal pulse that stays near silence under the old way, opens at the reveal, and lands the payoff and the mark on downbeats. Clicks only on real cursor clicks, soft key ticks on typed lines, one riser into the payoff. (Delivered silent offline — see BRIEF.)

## B — BEFORE / AFTER
- **Device:** Life before and after. **Form:** Split screen to one.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** Left half drowns in tabs, tickets and a clock; right half is calm, one window, one sentence. The divider is a cursor that drags the calm across the whole frame. Warm and human, less cinematic, easier to follow cold.
- **First two seconds:** Split frame — left: 14 tabs juddering; right: a single blinking cursor on bone white. The left side is already failing.
- **Beats:** split → left fails → cursor drags → one workspace → three verbs → payoff → claim → end card.
- **Look:** Bone paper #F2EDE3 ground, ink #141310, accent #FF5A1F. Editorial, printed — risks the banned cream-paper look if handled lazily.
- **Sound:** Left: muted office chatter and key clatter. Right: the same room, quiet, with the 120 BPM pulse underneath.

## C — TYPE AS CINEMA
- **Device:** Type carries the argument. **Form:** Cut on the beat.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** Giant kinetic type on colour fields cuts every half-bar — YOU DESCRIBE / IT DESIGNS / IT CODES / IT SHIPS — with the product flashing inside the letterforms. Bold and shareable, but it tells more than it shows; the proof is thin.
- **First two seconds:** YOU DESCRIBE fills 90% of the width on molten orange, cut on the first beat to a sentence being typed inside the counter of the O.
- **Beats:** hook line → four verb cuts → product flash → claim → end card.
- **Look:** Three flat colour fields (charcoal, bone, molten) in rotation, Montserrat 900 at 180 px, no UI except flashes inside type.
- **Sound:** Cut on every beat of a 128 BPM track; type hits as percussion.

**Pick:** A — the only one where the viewer watches the product being made, which is the claim. B is the fallback if a warmer, human tone is wanted; C if a 15 s social cutdown is needed later.
